Made for students and first-jobbers who pay by UPI. No bank password. No UPI PIN. Kitna never touches your money.

Kitna (कितना) means “how much?”: the one question it answers for you every day.

The problem.

Paying by UPI doesn't feel like spending. Your money is spread across GPay, PhonePe, bank SMS and statements, and none of them agree on one number. Budget apps die because typing every chai is tiring.

Inside Kitna.

What it does.

  • It logs itself. Turn it on once. Your UPI and bank payments show up by themselves, sorted into food, travel, bills and more. No typing.
  • It matches your bank. The same payment can arrive as a UPI alert, an SMS and a statement line. Kitna counts it once and checks itself against your bank balance.
  • It tells you one number. How much you can spend today, after your bills, your plans like Friday's dinner, and a safety cushion you choose.
  • You can just ask. “Is mahine food pe kitna gaya?” Ask in English, Hindi or Hinglish, by typing or by voice.
  • Friends pay you back. Split the bill and Kitna counts only your share. When Rahul sends his ₹600 on UPI, he is ticked off by himself.
  • It speaks up only when it matters. A bill due tomorrow, a late refund, a double charge. At most three reminders a day, and a short note on Sunday.
